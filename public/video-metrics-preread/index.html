Video extraction — where it stands and the metric we report

Annas · for the metrics-alignment session · internal only, not MS-facing

Where video stands

The video analysis found one core problem: extraction pulls in too many claims that are not worth scoring — about 20% on video vs 12% on modules. Video's higher "could not verify" rate at validation (26% vs 8%) is mostly the same problem surfacing a stage later: over 80% of the unverifiable claims trace back to over-extraction. So the two problems are one, the fix is at extraction, and we ruled out broadening grounding for video.

The metric

Headline today: non-falsifiable rate

Definition: the share of extracted claims judged not falsifiable — not a specific, checkable fact that could go out of date.

How measured: 5-model consensus per claim, checked against human review. Same instrument for video and modules, so the comparison is apples to apples.

Why this one: it is the number already shared (the roadmap slide, the tickets), so it keeps the story consistent while the extraction rubric is being ratified.

What "worth scoring" means — the rubric's dimensions

When the headline changes. Measured with the full rubric, video reads ~28% on our 130-claim sample vs modules ~13% (re-measured under the same judge, 7/13) — so the gap holds under the stricter test, and the extra junk it catches is almost entirely on the video side. We keep 20-vs-12 as the reported headline for now because it is what was already shared. One gate remains before the rubric-based rate takes over: the raw-vs-sample question (raw video output read ~13% on 10 videos — the sample may over-weight junk); this week's A/B re-extraction settles it.

A lower rate must not come from extracting less

Where reproducibility fits. Keith's number (run-to-run overlap) answers a different question: is the extractor stable enough that any of these measurements can be trusted. It is a prerequisite tracked in his write-up, not a competing quality number — his percentages and the 20% should never be compared or blended.

Progress so far

  • Baseline established: video 20% vs modules 12% (130-claim sample, consensus checked against human review).
  • Diagnosis confirmed: over 80% of video's could-not-verify traces to over-extraction; genuine Learn coverage gap ~3 of 34. One fix addresses both problems.
  • Extraction rubric drafted (from the 7/6 alignment) — to be ratified together.
  • Eval harness stood up — to be reviewed and aligned with Keith's shared harness and the golden set.

What's next

  1. Lock the rubric — it is the definition of "worth scoring," so the metric only means something once it's agreed.
  2. Ratify the eval harness with Keith (shared harness, golden set).
  3. Run the video extraction bake-off, scored on this metric with the recall guardrail.
  4. Report the number over time as it moves from 20% toward 12%.
The honest dependency. The metric is only as good as the agreed rubric and the golden set — human ground truth is currently 35 graded video claims, so the number carries small-sample uncertainty until the golden set grows.
